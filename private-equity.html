01 — Private equity

From data room to exit.

We build agentic systems that screen deals, underwrite them the way your investment committee would, watch every portfolio company, and weigh each one against the rest of the fund.

A portfolio of companies

Buy well, pay down the debt, build the business.

A buyout fund is a portfolio of private companies, often a dozen or two, each bought with a controlling stake and a meaningful amount of debt. That debt is paid down from the company's own free cash flow. So the return comes from three places: growing earnings, paying down the debt, and the price a buyer pays at exit. The agent separates the three, because only the first two are in the fund's hands.

The valuation work rhymes with public equities: normalized earnings, return on capital, cash conversion. The inputs are harder. There's no market price to lean on and no public filings, just data rooms, management accounts and lender reports, and adjusted EBITDA that has to be tested line by line. With leverage built in, the downside matters as much as the upside. Can the company keep servicing its debt if the plan slips or rates rise?

As with our other systems, the agent gives confidence ranges, not verdicts. And it judges every deal against the fund. Does it spread risk across sectors, exit years and debt structures, or stack more of the same?

What it does on every deal.

The work a careful deal team and portfolio operations team would do, from first look to exit.

  1. 01

    Reads the data room

    Teasers and CIMs, financial statements, quality-of-earnings reports, customer contracts, credit agreements and monthly management packs, with every figure linked back to its source.

  2. 02

    Finds the real earnings

    Every add-back and adjustment to EBITDA is tested: one-offs that recur, owner costs that won't go away, pro forma savings not yet earned. Then it checks how much of that EBITDA actually turns into cash.

  3. 03

    Values the business

    The entry multiple is set against comparable deals and public peers, return on capital and growth, to see how much of the price is paying for a plan that hasn't happened yet.

  4. 04

    Tests the debt

    How much debt the business can carry, how fast free cash flow can pay it down, covenant headroom, and interest coverage if rates rise or earnings dip.

  5. 05

    Stress-tests the plan

    Every value-creation plan rests on a few assumptions about margins, pricing, add-on acquisitions or new markets. The agent finds the ones the return depends on most and tests how far they can slip.

  6. 06

    Looks for trouble years ahead

    Customer and supplier concentration, working capital that keeps absorbing cash, capital spending the business has put off, reliance on a few key people, and competitors moving in.

  7. 07

    Watches every portfolio company

    Monthly packs are pulled into one consistent model. KPIs are tracked against plan and covenant headroom against the lender's tests, with early warnings when a company starts to drift.

  8. 08

    Models the range of outcomes

    Scenarios for growth, margins, rates and exit multiples give a confidence range on money back and annual return, and the probability of losing capital, updated as each month's numbers arrive.

  9. 09

    Balances the fund and reports on it

    Exposure by sector, exit year and debt structure, including risks companies share without looking alike. Then it drafts the quarterly reports to your investors from the same numbers.
