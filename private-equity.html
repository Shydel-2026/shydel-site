A portfolio of companies

The data room instead of EDGAR. The same eighteen stages after that.

A buyout fund is a portfolio of private companies bought with control and with debt that is paid down from free cash flow. The return comes from earnings growth, debt paydown and the exit multiple, and only the first two are in the fund's hands. There are no public filings, so the document tools read the data room: audited accounts, management accounts and the quality-of-earnings report, with every add-back tested and every figure cited to the page.

Private equity has its own lane on the board and its own scope in memory, seeded with your fund's deal criteria, the rules and skills for each stage. Eighteen stages, in order.

The agent's eighteen stages, read for a deal. Stage two is the data room and the quality of earnings; the debt tables test the proposed structure; the decision weighs the deal against the fund.

1
Idea sourcing and screening optional

Screens deal flow: teasers and CIMs against the fund's mandate on size, sector and price.

2
Filings and accounting quality

The data room instead of EDGAR: audited accounts, management accounts and the quality-of-earnings report; every add-back to EBITDA tested.

3
Balance sheet

The tangible capital the business needs, working capital, and what the lenders can claim.

4
Cash flow statement

Free cash flow available to pay down acquisition debt, year by year.

5
Income statement

Normalised EBITDA and EBIT after owner costs and one-offs; margins.

6
Debt tables

The proposed structure: senior, mezzanine, covenants, coverage and paydown under stress.

7
Commitments and contingencies

Earn-outs, leases, warranties and litigation inherited with the deal.

8
Receivables and provision for credit losses

Receivable quality and concentration; cash that may not arrive.

9
Market and segment analysis

The end markets, their growth and the company's share of them.

10
Inventory analysis

Inventory turns and obsolescence, where they apply.

11
Leases and real estate analysis

Owned property that can be sold and leased back; leases that bind the plan.

12
Depreciation analysis

Deferred maintenance that the plan will have to fund.

13
Business quality and moat

What protects margins through the hold period.

14
Management and governance

The team that stays, their rollover, and the incentive plan.

15
Valuation

Entry multiple against comparable deals; money back and annual return across exit scenarios.

16
Risk and margin of safety

Leverage, customer concentration and the chance of losing capital.

17
Decision, sizing and portfolio fit

Sector overlap, exit year and shared rate risk across the fund.

18
Monitoring and selling

Monthly packs, covenant headroom and KPIs against plan; the exit case.

What it looks like in the Shydel Agent.

Private equity has its own lane on the board and its own scope in memory. The stages, the scorecard and the learning loop are the same as on every other asset class; the playbook is your fund's.

In the app

Write your fund's rules in.

The deal playbook is your fund's: write a rule in one sentence, give the details and the why, and tag it to the asset class and the stage where it applies. It is pushed to the agent the next time a deal reaches that stage.

Read the data room.

The document tools fetch, search and read the PDFs a deal comes with, the way they read annual reports for public companies: every figure is cited to the page it came from.

Every stage, written up with its sources.

Each stage ends with a finding, marked as supporting the thesis, neutral, or skipped with the reason. For a buyout that means the quality of earnings, the debt structure and the commitments are each written up and cited.
