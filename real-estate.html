02 — Real estate

Real estate is a portfolio. Run it like one.

We build agentic systems that underwrite every property, project and lease the way your team does, and weigh each one against everything you already own.

Every property is a position

One fund, many strategies, a few cities.

A real estate fund or REIT is a portfolio like any other. It may own commercial and residential buildings outright, usually concentrated in a handful of cities. It may buy land and develop it, either to sell or to hold and lease. Each strategy has its own economics and its own risks, and every new asset changes the risk of the whole.

The agent is trained on how your firm underwrites: target yields, cost and occupancy assumptions, development hurdles. Like the rest of our systems, it doesn't stop at pass or fail. It looks for what could break the plan years out and gives a confidence range that each asset delivers what it was underwritten to do.

Then it asks whether the asset makes the portfolio better. Will it add to funds from operations, spread risk across cities and property types, or pile more onto exposure you already carry?

What it does on every asset.

The work a careful asset manager or acquisitions analyst would do, across every property and project, each time new numbers arrive.

  1. 01

    Reads every document

    Leases, rent rolls, operating statements, appraisals, construction contracts and loan agreements, with every term linked back to the page it came from.

  2. 02

    Finds the real cash flow

    Net operating income is cleaned of one-off items, then carried through to FFO and AFFO, the measures of cash the portfolio actually generates after the spending needed to keep buildings competitive.

  3. 03

    Underwrites acquisitions

    Price against replacement cost and comparable sales, going-in yield against your hurdle, and in-place rents against the market, to see whether there's upside or a rent roll that's about to reset lower.

  4. 04

    Tracks development

    Land, permits, construction budget, cost to complete and schedule, checked against each new cost report. Overruns and delays are flagged while there is still time to act.

  5. 05

    Optimizes operations

    Operating cost per unit and per square foot against comparable buildings, occupancy, rent growth and tenant retention, to find where each property is leaving income on the table.

  6. 06

    Watches the leases

    Expiries bunching into the same year, reliance on a few large tenants, and the credit of the tenants themselves, all tracked across the portfolio.

  7. 07

    Looks for trouble years ahead

    New supply coming to a submarket, construction and operating costs outrunning rents, loans that will refinance at higher rates, and shifts in demand for a property type.

  8. 08

    Models the range of outcomes

    Scenarios for rents, occupancy, costs and exit values give a confidence range that each asset delivers its underwriting, and the probability of loss, updated as new figures come in.

  9. 09

    Balances the portfolio

    Exposure by city, property type, strategy and tenant, and how each asset moves with the rest. It also weighs whether to hold, sell, develop or lease, to find the move that does the most for the portfolio.
