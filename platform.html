The process

Research in levels. Only the few that pass go deeper.

A screen brings candidates. Level 1 verifies the numbers and rejects most of them. The rest get segments and the industry, then the full eighteen-stage analysis. Your top picks are stress-tested, and the whole book is reviewed as one business.

Top picks · the card

A score that leads with the margin of safety.

Every company on the board carries a score out of ten. The margin of safety weighs most; return on capital, cash conversion, the moat and the risks follow.

Above your threshold, a buy signal. Below the other, a sell. Moving a name into top picks is always your call.

Level 4 · the stress test

The thesis, argued against itself.

A challenger, an advocate and an arbiter each score the company blind, then debate every input — growth, return on new capital, the chance of a permanent loss — round by round, until they converge on one score.

The challenger concedes what the evidence supports and holds the rest. The arbiter records what the debate turned on.

Level 5 · the portfolio

The book, read as one business.

Margins, free cash flow, debt and the earnings yield of the whole portfolio, before and after the proposed changes. What would change the plan, what to watch, and when cash is the right answer.

Nothing is executed. These are recommendations for your decision.

The value framework

What a business earns, what it owns, and what you pay for it.

Every company, financial or operating, goes through the same framework: Graham and Dodd's security analysis, with Greenwald's two values and Buffett's view of quality on top. Your firm's rules adjust it. Nothing replaces the margin of safety.

01 — Earning power

What the business earns in a normal year.

Seven years of statements, verified against the annual report. One-off gains and charges come out line by line, the margin is taken across the cycle, and depreciation is tested against what the business really spends to stand still. The result is owner earnings, not reported earnings per share.

Stages 2–5
02 — Two values

Earnings power, and the assets behind it.

Earnings power value capitalises normal earnings at the cost of capital, with no growth. Asset value asks what it would cost to rebuild the business, or what it would fetch in liquidation. Earnings power well above the assets is the mark of a franchise, and then the work is to prove it lasts.

Stages 3, 5 and 13
03 — Growth that earns its keep

Growth counts only above the cost of capital.

New capital has to earn more than it costs, or growth destroys value. The agent measures the marginal return on capital by segment, and grounds the growth outlook in the industry and the segments, never in analysts' projections alone.

Stages 9 and 15
04 — Value as a range

A range of outcomes, each with its odds.

Intrinsic value is a set of scenarios from a discounted cash flow at a cost of capital built several ways, not a single number. The decision rests on the conservative end of the range, and the chance of a permanent loss of capital is estimated and written down.

Stages 15–16
05 — The margin of safety

The conservative value, against today's price.

The gap between them is 40% of every score. The earnings yield has to clear the government ten-year in the company's own currency, pulled live. Every thesis ends with a buy-below price: the level at which the margin is wide enough to act.

Stages 16–17
06 — Quality and risk

How far the margin of safety can be trusted.

Return on capital, cash conversion, the balance sheet and its debt, the moat, and management's record of allocating capital, including whether buybacks were done below value. They decide whether a cheap price is an opportunity or a warning.

Stages 6, 13 and 14
The playbooks

The first stage picks the playbook. The framework stays the same.

The first stage classifies the company, and from then on that playbook's rules apply at every stage. A company can carry two: a quality franchise bought as a cyclical, a holding company read as deep value.

Graham and DoddDeep value

Cheap on normalised earnings or below book, NAV or net current assets. Valued on earning power and the assets; growth counts for little.

Buffett and GreenwaldQuality franchise

High returns on tangible capital for years, pricing power, customers who stay. Valued on the expected return at today's price.

GreenblattSpecial situations

Spin-offs, liquidations, rights offerings, mergers. Valued on the sum of the parts or the spread against what breaks the deal.

Through the cycleCyclicals and turnarounds

A bad year priced as permanent. Valued on mid-cycle margins, with the assets as the floor and the debt tested at the trough.

Book valueFinancials

Banks, insurers and holding companies. Valued on book value, net asset value and return on equity, with capital and reserves read at the debt stage.

FisherGrowth

The fifteen points on the business and its management, held to the same price discipline as everything else.

Two examples

One framework, applied to a financial and to an operating business.

A financial · E-L Financial (ELF.TO)

Valued on what it owns.

A holding company is not valued on EBIT. The agent reads E-L's own annual reports, values the investment portfolio at market and the life insurer at book, and checks the insurer's capital. The net equity value is C$27.37 a share against an C$18.46 price: about 67 cents on the dollar, for an asset base with no debt that has compounded about 11% a year for a decade.

The discount is the margin of safety, and the compounding is earned even if it never closes. Score 8.3, a buy signal; buy below C$21.5.

An operating business · Trigano (TRI.PA)

Valued on what it earns through the cycle.

Europe's largest motorhome and caravan maker, after a destocking year the market priced as permanent. On its seven-year average margin of about 10.6%, it earns roughly €388M of operating profit, so it trades at 5.3 times EBIT and about 6 times earnings, with net cash.

Returns on tangible capital of 27% reported and 39% normalised held up through the trough: a narrow moat, not none. A conservative appraisal of about €196 against a €126 price leaves a wide margin of safety. Score 9.7 after the stress test; buy below €140.
