03 — Fixed income

The return is fixed. The risk isn't.

For a bond the agent starts where a lender starts: with the downside. It reads the same eighteen stages for coverage, seniority, collateral and covenants, pulls the hurdle rate from the government curve, and records what you would recover if the issuer failed.

Protection first

The same stages, read the way a lender reads them.

A bond's return is fixed and known; the loss if the issuer fails is not. So the agent reads the eighteen stages with the downside first: interest coverage on normalised earnings and again in a bad year, every claim ahead of yours in the debt tables, the collateral and the cushion of equity beneath the issue, and the covenants that protect it.

Hurdle rates come from the source, not a spreadsheet. Government yields and inflation are pulled live from the Treasury, the Bank of Canada, the ECB and the OECD, and the ten-year in the issuer's currency is the rate every yield has to beat.

Fixed income shares the board and the memory, with its own rules and skills: covenant review, convertible and warrant terms, high-yield credit analysis. The cards below are illustrative: the agent's format, applied to a senior secured bond. Click a stage to see what it does there.

Eighteen stages, in order.

The agent's eighteen stages, read for an issuer. The debt tables, commitments and covenants carry the most weight; the decision is about the cushion beneath the claim.

1
Idea sourcing and screening optional

Screens the issuer universe on yield against the hurdle, coverage and seniority; new issues, exchanges and distressed names.

2
Filings and accounting quality

Pulls the filings and the offering documents: the indenture or credit agreement alongside the 10-K. The issuer is classified like any company.

3
Balance sheet

Asset coverage: what stands behind the claim, tangible assets first.

4
Cash flow statement

Cash generation available to service and repay debt after capital spending.

5
Income statement

Normalised EBIT and interest coverage, then coverage again in a bad year.

6
Debt tables

The whole capital structure in order of seniority: maturities, rates, security, guarantees and covenants.

7
Commitments and contingencies

Off-balance-sheet claims that rank alongside or ahead of yours.

8
Receivables and provision for credit losses

Working-capital quality that can drain liquidity before a maturity.

9
Market and segment analysis

Which businesses generate the cash that pays the coupon, and how cyclical they are.

10
Inventory analysis

Inventory that can turn into a liquidity need; skipped where it does not apply.

11
Leases and real estate analysis

Lease obligations that rank ahead; property that is pledged or could be.

12
Depreciation analysis

Whether maintenance spending is being deferred to flatter coverage.

13
Business quality and moat

The durability of the cash flows over the life of the bond.

14
Management and governance

Dividend recapitalisations, asset sales, and how creditors were treated before.

15
Valuation

Yield to maturity against the government curve, the spread for the risk, and recovery if the issuer defaults.

16
Risk and margin of safety

Probability of default and loss given default; the cushion of equity and junior claims beneath you.

17
Decision, sizing and portfolio fit

Issuer and sector concentration, duration, and whether this default risk rises with the rest of the book.

18
Monitoring and selling

Covenant headroom, rating actions and refinancing dates, re-checked as filings arrive.

Memory and skills

What the agent brings to each stage.

Credit has its own scope: standards for senior securities, high-yield analysis, covenant review. Most of it is tagged to the debt stage, where a bond is actually judged, and it is pushed alongside the firm-wide rules.

EpisodesDecisions, corrections and approvals, recorded as they happen.
RulesValue and process rules, each with a why and a confidence.
SkillsWritten procedures, one per task: normalise earnings, read covenants, value a REIT.
KnowledgeFramework notes and company files the agent keeps between sessions.

Equity-only rules are left out when the agent works a bond; your credit committee's standards become rules in this scope.

In the platform

What it looks like in the Shydel Agent.

Fixed income sits on the same board as everything else, with its own lane, its own rules and the macro tools that set the hurdle rate.

In the app

Owned, weighed against the whole book.

Bonds, bond funds and cash sit in the fixed income lane next to the equity and real estate lanes, each with its weight in the book. Weight, duration and issuer exposure are judged against the whole book, not the bond alone.

Credit rules, kept at the debt stage.

Scope the memory to fixed income and the debt stage opens onto the credit library: standards for senior securities, covenant review, convertible terms, high-yield analysis. These are pushed to the agent when it reaches the debt tables of an issuer.

A credit skill, opened.
