04 — Fixed income

The return is fixed. The risk isn't.

We build agentic systems for credit teams that start where a lender starts: with the downside. Trained on your credit framework, the agent asks one question of every issue, and keeps asking it as new filings arrive. Can this company fail to pay us back?

Protection first

A bond's upside is capped. Its downside isn't.

An equity investor is paid for being right about what a business could become. A bondholder is paid a fixed coupon, and the return to maturity can be worked out in a line. What can't be worked out so easily is the loss if something unforeseen goes wrong. So the agent puts its effort where credit risk actually lives: whether the issuer can keep paying, and what you recover if it can't.

As with equities, nothing is pass/fail. Thin coverage can be acceptable with hard collateral and a deep cushion of equity beneath you. Strong coverage counts for less if you're last in line. The agent weighs these against each other, looks ahead for what could weaken them, and turns the result into a confidence range around repayment rather than a single rating.

And no bond is judged alone. Credit losses tend to arrive together, in the same sectors and the same downturns. So the agent also asks whether an issuer's risk of default moves with what you already hold, and how confident it is that the bond makes the whole portfolio safer, not just that it will be repaid.

What it does on every issue.

The work a careful credit analyst would do, on every issuer you hold or follow, each time something changes.

  1. 01

    Reads every document

    Annual and quarterly reports, prospectuses, indentures and credit agreements, pulled as soon as they're filed. The terms that matter are extracted and linked back to the clause they came from.

  2. 02

    Tests the coverage

    Interest coverage is measured on normalized earnings, stripped of one-off items, and then again in a bad year. A company that covers its interest comfortably only in good times isn't a safe borrower.

  3. 03

    Maps the capital structure

    Every claim ahead of yours and behind it is laid out in order, including leases, pensions and debt at subsidiaries that can quietly rank ahead of a parent company's bonds.

  4. 04

    Values the collateral

    What's pledged, how the liens are structured and what the assets would realistically fetch in a forced sale, not what they're carried at on the balance sheet.

  5. 05

    Measures the equity cushion

    The market value of the stock beneath the issue shows how much value the company can lose before bondholders are touched. It is tracked as the share price moves, not once a quarter.

  6. 06

    Reads the covenants

    What protection the indenture really gives, how much headroom is left, and where the gaps are that would let assets or cash move out of reach of bondholders.

  7. 07

    Looks for trouble years ahead

    Maturities bunching into a refinancing wall, free cash flow being absorbed by working capital, spending the business will have to make, and competition starting to erode margins.

  8. 08

    Models default and recovery

    A range of scenarios for the chance of default and how much would be recovered if it happened, giving a probability of loss with an honest range around it. Updated as new figures come in.

  9. 09

    Fits it to the portfolio

    Issuer and sector concentration, duration and spread, and whether its default risk rises in the same downturns as the rest of the book. It also compares the issuer's other bonds and loans, to find the claim that offers the most protection for the yield.
