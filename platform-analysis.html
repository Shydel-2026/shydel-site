The analysis

Eighteen stages, in order. No skipping without a reason.

Every company goes through the same principled sequence: the filings and their quality first, then each financial statement, then the business, then the decision. The agent cannot leave a stage without written findings and citations, and it cannot record a thesis until risk and margin of safety are done. A stage that does not apply is skipped explicitly, with the reason kept.

Goal

Run a full principled company analysis of Open Text Corporation (OTEX.TO) for the portfolio, which currently holds it on the watchlist. Classify the company, work every analysis stage with cited findings, write thesis/valuation/scorecard cards, ground the decision in current portfolio weights, and record the thesis and analysis on the board.

The first stage picks the playbook. The agent classifies the company as deep value, quality franchise, growth, special situation, turnaround, cyclical or financial, and from then on only the rules and skills written for that type are pushed into working memory, stage by stage.

Deep value / asset play (Graham)Quality franchise (Buffett / Greenwald)Growth (Fisher)Special situation (Greenblatt)Turnaround / distressedCyclical / commodityFinancial (bank / insurer)
1
Idea sourcing and screening optional

Screens on EV/EBIT, return on tangible capital and growth, plus a full-text search of filings for spin-offs, recapitalisations and other special situations. Optional: skipped when you hand it the company.

2
Filings and accounting quality

Pulls the 10-K and 10-Q from EDGAR, classifies the company (deep value, franchise, growth, special situation, turnaround, cyclical or financial) and tests whether reported earnings match cash earnings.

3
Balance sheet

Tangible against intangible assets, net working capital, net fixed assets, and the capital the business really needs.

4
Cash flow statement

Operating cash flow against net income, capital spending against depreciation, free cash flow every year.

5
Income statement

Normalised operating income with one-offs removed; operating margin and its trend.

6
Debt tables

Maturities, rates and covenants, and what refinancing will cost.

7
Commitments and contingencies

Leases, guarantees, litigation and purchase obligations that sit off the balance sheet.

8
Receivables and provision for credit losses

Receivables against sales, their ageing, and whether provisions keep pace.

9
Market and segment analysis

Where the sales and profits come from, by segment and geography, and how each is moving.

10
Inventory analysis

Inventory against sales and cost of sales; obsolescence and write-downs. Skipped, with the reason kept, at an asset-light company.

11
Leases and real estate analysis

Operating leases, owned property, and what they are worth against what they are carried at.

12
Depreciation analysis

Economic against book depreciation, and the gap between capital spending and the charge.

13
Business quality and moat

Switching costs, scale, brands, and returns on capital through a cycle.

14
Management and governance

The capital-allocation record: buybacks above or below value, dividends, acquisitions, pay, control and related parties.

15
Valuation

Earnings power value, normalised multiples, and bear, base and bull scenarios with their probabilities.

16
Risk and margin of safety

What breaks the thesis, the chance of a permanent loss, and the price that leaves room for error.

17
Decision, sizing and portfolio fit

Verdict, target weight, buy-below and trim-above prices, and whether the position diversifies the book or adds to exposure you already carry.

18
Monitoring and selling

Sell triggers, what would change the agent's mind, and what to watch in the next filing.

Same stages, different playbook

What changes from one asset class to the next.

The scorecard

Reasons for and against, weighed the way you weigh them.

At the end of the analysis the agent writes three cards: the thesis, the valuation and the scorecard. The scorecard lists every reason for and against the company, each with an effect from −1 to +1 and a measure it belongs to. The measures carry standard weights, price first. The result is a final score out of ten, the role the company would play in the portfolio, a verdict, and the chance of a permanent loss.

Price EV/EBIT and P/E30%
Return on tangible capital ROIC25%
Operating margin business quality15%
Growth sales and operating income12%
Balance sheet and debt10%
Management and capital allocation5%
Other3%

Price and return on capital are required; no other measure may weigh more than price. The valuation card holds the bear, base and bull values with their probabilities, the probability-weighted value, and the buy-below and trim-above prices. The thesis card holds the verdict, the conviction and the sell triggers.
