The board

Every company in the book, at one of four stages.

The portfolio is a board with one swimlane per asset class and four columns: Watchlist, Researching, Owned and Exiting. Above the lanes sits the whole book: value, weight by asset class and the allocation bar, priced from FMP, then Yahoo, else the price you entered.

Public companies, from watchlist to owned.

Banks and insurers, routed to their own lane.

A bank or insurer is placed in the financials lane the moment it is added, and the financial playbook (book value, underwriting, return on equity) applies from the first stage. Return on tangible capital is replaced by return on invested capital where tangible capital is not meaningful.

Owned, weighed against the whole book.

Bonds, bond funds and cash, each with its weight in the book. Weight, duration and issuer exposure are judged against the whole book, next to the equity and real estate lanes.

REITs on the board.

REITs and property companies, analysed through the same eighteen stages with the real estate rules: property counted in the capital base, AFFO as the cash measure, the lease schedule read in stage eleven.

A card, opened

Every holding, in depth.

Open any card for its weight and value, price, EV/EBIT and P/E, return on tangible capital with its margin and turnover, then the statements year by year: sales, margins, capital and cash flow. Below it sit the thesis, the scorecard, the sources and the memory the agent used.
