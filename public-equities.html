01 — Public equities

An analyst trained on your framework.

The Shydel Agent works every public company through eighteen ordered stages, from the filings to the decision. At each one it pushes your firm's rules into its working memory, and at the end it records the thesis, the valuation and the scorecard on your board. Every number cites the filing it came from.

Your framework, not a checklist

The first stage picks the playbook. The scorecard weighs it your way.

In the first stage the agent classifies the company: deep value, quality franchise, growth, special situation, turnaround, cyclical or financial. From then on only the rules and skills written for that type are pushed into its working memory, stage by stage. A Graham value shop and a Fisher growth fund get different rules at every step, from the same library.

Almost no company passes every screen, so the scorecard does not pass or fail. Every reason for and against gets an effect from −1 to +1, weighted by what your firm counts most: price (EV/EBIT and P/E) 30%, return on tangible capital 25%, operating margin 15%, growth 12%, balance sheet 10%, management 5%. The result is a score out of ten, a role in the portfolio, a verdict and the chance of a permanent loss.

Eighteen stages, in order.

The same ordered analysis on every public company. The agent cannot leave a stage without written findings and citations, and it cannot record a thesis until risk and margin of safety are done.

1
Idea sourcing and screening optional

Screens on EV/EBIT, return on tangible capital and growth, plus a full-text search of filings for spin-offs, recapitalisations and other special situations. Optional: skipped when you hand it the company.

2
Filings and accounting quality

Pulls the 10-K and 10-Q from EDGAR, classifies the company (deep value, franchise, growth, special situation, turnaround, cyclical or financial) and tests whether reported earnings match cash earnings.

3
Balance sheet

Tangible against intangible assets, net working capital, net fixed assets, and the capital the business really needs.

4
Cash flow statement

Operating cash flow against net income, capital spending against depreciation, free cash flow every year.

5
Income statement

Normalised operating income with one-offs removed; operating margin and its trend.

6
Debt tables

Maturities, rates and covenants, and what refinancing will cost.

7
Commitments and contingencies

Leases, guarantees, litigation and purchase obligations that sit off the balance sheet.

8
Receivables and provision for credit losses

Receivables against sales, their ageing, and whether provisions keep pace.

9
Market and segment analysis

Where the sales and profits come from, by segment and geography, and how each is moving.

10
Inventory analysis

Inventory against sales and cost of sales; obsolescence and write-downs. Skipped, with the reason kept, at an asset-light company.

11
Leases and real estate analysis

Operating leases, owned property, and what they are worth against what they are carried at.

12
Depreciation analysis

Economic against book depreciation, and the gap between capital spending and the charge.

13
Business quality and moat

Switching costs, scale, brands, and returns on capital through a cycle.

14
Management and governance

The capital-allocation record: buybacks above or below value, dividends, acquisitions, pay, control and related parties.

15
Valuation

Earnings power value, normalised multiples, and bear, base and bull scenarios with their probabilities.

16
Risk and margin of safety

What breaks the thesis, the chance of a permanent loss, and the price that leaves room for error.

17
Decision, sizing and portfolio fit

Verdict, target weight, buy-below and trim-above prices, and whether the position diversifies the book or adds to exposure you already carry.

18
Monitoring and selling

Sell triggers, what would change the agent's mind, and what to watch in the next filing.

Memory and skills

What the agent brings to each stage.

At each stage the agent is pushed the rules and skills tagged for that stage, filtered to the company's playbook: a deep-value holding gets Graham's tests, a franchise gets the moat and return-on-capital work. Below, examples from the library. Click a skill for what it does.

EpisodesDecisions, corrections and approvals, recorded as they happen.
RulesValue and process rules, each with a why and a confidence.
SkillsWritten procedures, one per task: normalise earnings, read covenants, value a REIT.
KnowledgeFramework notes and company files the agent keeps between sessions.

Skills are procedures the agent follows. Rules are standing value and process rules; each has a why, a confidence and a lifecycle, and none changes the agent's behaviour until you approve it.
